Product · In development

Everything it takes to build a pinball machine.

Deadbounce is a studio for people building their own pinball machines. You design the playfield in 3D at real size, build the game’s rules without writing code, give it a display and sound, and play it. Then you take the cut plan, a drill template and a wiring plan to the workshop.

I’m designing and building it end to end, from the interface and the 3D editor to the physics engine and the files a machine shop reads.

The work

A homebrew machine usually means five programs that don’t talk to each other. Deadbounce keeps one game, from the first post to the last wire.

Design

Build it the way real games are built.

Parts go together as they do under the glass: posts screwed into T-nuts, flippers on their mounting plate with the coil under the board, ramps on standoffs, steel guides on feet where the ball can’t reach them. Design checks flag lower-third problems, such as a lane that could trap a ball.

Rules

Write the game without writing code.

Nineteen rule features, from lane sets and target banks to multiball and wizard modes, each shown in plain English and as the exact Mission Pinball Framework configuration it generates. The test suite plays every one, switch by switch, in real games.

Play

Test what you’ll actually build.

The ball runs on Deadbounce’s own physics engine, built from the same geometry the studio cuts and prints, with your rules and display live. Visual Pinball X stays available as an optional player and export.

Build and wire

Take it to the workshop.

Every hole, slot and insert pocket comes out as a DXF and SVG cut plan with a tiled drill template that prints at true size. The controller plan gives every switch, coil and light an address, with a wiring sheet, a power estimate and a safety checklist.

Inside the studio

Each step is one page, with the way on in the corner.

The machine on the home page is built to Deadbounce’s measurements: the board, the flipper spacing, the slingshots and the apron.
