Sonic’s slingshots, with springs on top.

3D printed pinball mod On sale

The slingshot plastics on Jersey Jack’s Sonic the Hedgehog are flat. I wanted bounce springs on top of them, like the ones in the games, at the same size and on the same posts as the stock plastics, with no wiring. So I scanned the original, modelled a pair to match, drew a new face for it, and printed it in four colours.

Then I kept breaking it and fixing it. Six versions later nothing on it is glued. Every joint is a screw into metal, and any part can be reprinted and swapped on its own. The sixth version is fitted in the game, and the pair is on sale.

Three parts I like most

Where most of the effort went: a print trick, a joint and a box.

The face

Printing the face upside down.

The first plates printed face up, and you could follow every pass of the nozzle across the red. So I turned the part over. The face now prints straight onto a textured build plate and takes the plate’s finish, and the red and gold meet on the very first layer.

That showed up a new problem: small gaps wherever two gold squares touched at a corner. A corner is a point, and a nozzle cannot fill a point. The fix went into the pattern itself. No two squares share a corner. The biggest square is 3.7 mm in a 4.4 mm cell, which leaves 1 mm of red between diagonal neighbours.

The joint

Getting rid of the glue.

The original had realistic open-coil springs. Air balls snapped them off. Version 2 made the springs solid. Version 3 bolted them to the plate. That left the caps glued on, which made them the obvious next thing to fail.

Version 4 ran one screw down through the whole stack. Version 5 hid that screw under a twist-lock medallion. Version 6 threw most of that away. A brass insert is melted into the underside of each cap, and one M3 screw comes up through the plate and the spring into it. One printed cap, one screw, no glue.

The box

A box that turns into a stand.

The stand I built to photograph the slings became the packaging. The base has six posts at the slings’ own hole positions. The lid clicks on over five ridges, and six sleeves inside it stop 0.3 mm above the slings so they cannot lift off the posts in transit.

Take the lid off and it is a display stand. It says SONIC PINBALL across the front, so the original plastics have somewhere to live once the new ones are in the game.

The first slice of the box came in at 4 h 25 min. Tuning it in Bambu Studio got that to 3 h 27 min without touching the faces people actually look at.

How it got here

Six versions and a box. Each one fixed the weakest part of the one before.

  1. Original

    One-piece plate with realistic open springs and star caps.

    The starting idea: stock size, springs on top, no wiring.

  2. Version 2 · 10 November

    Solid springs, and mounting holes cut into the model with gold rings round them.

    Air balls were snapping the open springs. Eight face designs were drawn here, and the checker won.

  3. Version 3 · 11 November

    The plate prints face down. The springs become separate parts, bolted through the plate.

    Nozzle paths showed on the face, and the spring joint needed to be stronger.

  4. Version 4 · 12 November

    One screw through cap, spring and plate into a lock nut. The cap splits into a body and a glued medallion.

    Glued caps were the weak point.

  5. Version 5 · 12 November

    The medallion twist-locks over the screw head. No glue anywhere.

    Glue is not a repairable joint.

  6. Version 6 · 12 November

    A one-piece cap with a brass heat-set insert. The screw comes up from under the plate.

    Fewer parts, the same strength, and nothing to line up.

  7. The box · 12 November

    The display stand, reworked into click-shut packaging.

    The stand already held the slings exactly as the game does.

The final product

The final print, straight off the printer and onto the bench. Silk PLA for the slings, black for the box.

What I used

I modelled the slings in Fusion. The scripts and Claude came in afterwards, for the clean-up.

  • Autodesk Fusion, for the modelling
  • Revopoint MetroX 3D scanner, for the outline of the original plastic
  • Python (manifold3d, shapely) with Claude, for the clean-up work
  • Bambu Studio, for slicing and print tuning
  • Bambu Lab H2C, 0.4 mm nozzle, textured PEI plate
  • Bambu PLA Silk+ in red, gold, silver and blue. Black PLA for the box
  • three.js, for the renders and animations
  • M3 brass heat-set inserts and M3 × 20 countersunk screws
